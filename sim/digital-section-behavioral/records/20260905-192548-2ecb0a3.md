## Notes on what each experiment is and is not

- **B and C are logic checks, not entropy measurements.** They confirm
  the tests fire where the formulas say they should on a source
  constructed to fail; they say nothing about whether sky130's real
  entropy source would pass.
- **E is a floor under the false-alarm argument, not a verification of
  it.** 1,000,000 samples is ~2^19.9 -- vanishingly
  small against a per-sample false-alarm probability of 2^-40, so this
  run could not have observed a legitimate false alarm. It would have
  caught a gross implementation error (an off-by-one cutoff, a
  mis-sized window) that made the tests fire orders of magnitude too
  often, which is what it is for.
- **D's source is below the design's own `H` target** (`H` = 0.415 vs the
  0.5 target) and is still not caught, which is the honest behaviour of
  an SP 800-90B health test: it detects catastrophic failure, not
  gradual entropy loss. That is a property of the standard's test
  design, and it is why the block's entropy claim rests on the source
  model and (eventually) measured silicon, not on the health tests.
