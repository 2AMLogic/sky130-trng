## Sized N (acceptance criterion 5)

`N = ceil(M*Q_H0 / Q_ring(binding))` = ceil(0.005946 / 0.0001122) = **53** `ro_ring5` rings at `T_s` = 1 us.

`Q` is linear in `T_s`, and `N` is inversely linear in it, so the sizing is entirely hostage to the raw-rate row:

| raw rate | `T_s` | `Q_array` per ring | sized `N` (`ro_ring5`) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 Mbps | 0.1 us | 1.122e-05 | **530** |
| 1 Mbps | 1 us | 0.0001122 | **53** |
| 100 kbps | 10 us | 0.001122 | **6** |
| 10 kbps | 100 us | 0.01122 | **1** |
| 2 kbps (gf180-trng's own proposed row) | 500 us | 0.05611 | **1** |

### Stage count, measured rather than inherited

| point | `ro_ring5` `Q_ring` | `ro_ring11` `Q_ring` | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| ss / 125 degC / 1.62 V (slow/hot/low-supply) | 0.000224 | 1.927e-05 | 11.63x |
| tt / 27 degC / 1.8 V (nominal) | 0.0005719 | 2.041e-05 | 28.02x |
| ff / -40 degC / 1.98 V (fast/cold/high-supply) | 0.00118 | 2.435e-05 | 48.48x |

The naive expectation from the sizing law alone is that `sigma_1^2` grows with the stage count while `T_0` grows with it too, giving `Q ~ 1/n^2`. Using the *measured* `T_0` ratio at nominal (2.425 ns vs 6.431 ns) and `sigma_1^2 ~ n`, the predicted `Q` ratio at nominal is ~8.5x. The measured ratio is ~28x, i.e. ~3.3x more penalty than the law predicts, because the measured `sigma_1` *fell* going from 5 to 11 stages instead of rising as `sqrt(n)`. That discrepancy is real and consistent in sign at all three headline points, and this campaign does NOT explain it -- it is recorded as an open question, not smoothed over. Its direction is conservative for the conclusion drawn here (it makes the 11-stage ring look worse, and the conclusion is that 11 stages is expensive), so it does not soften the sizing verdict; it does mean the `ro_ring5`-vs-`ro_ring11` ratio itself should not be quoted as a device-physics constant.

### What the committed placeholder `N = 2` actually delivers

`H` below is the Baudet et al. bound evaluated at `Q_array = N * Q_ring(binding corner)`, i.e. the min-entropy per raw bit the **sizing model is willing to guarantee** at a 1 us sample period -- not a measured entropy and not an upper bound on the source's real behaviour.

Read the shape of the bound before reading the numbers: it saturates at `1 - 4/(pi^2 ln 2)` = 0.4153 as `Q -> 0`, so a badly undersized array does not read as `H = 0` here. The operative sizing statement is the `Q` requirement, not the `H` column.

| `N` (`ro_ring5`) | `Q_array` | guaranteed `H` | meets `H0` = 0.5? | meets `M*Q_H0`? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.0001122 | 0.4179 | no | no |
| 2 <- committed placeholder | 0.0002245 | 0.4205 | no | no |
| 4 | 0.0004489 | 0.4256 | no | no |
| 8 | 0.0008978 | 0.4357 | no | no |
| 16 | 0.001796 | 0.4553 | no | no |
| 32 | 0.003591 | 0.4926 | no | no |
| 36 <- bare `H0` | 0.00404 | 0.5015 | yes | no |
| 53 <- **sized, with margin** | 0.005948 | 0.5377 | yes | yes |

So the committed `N = 2` is **refuted, quantitatively**: at the measured entropy-binding corner it guarantees `H` = 0.4205 bit/raw-bit against a declared `H0` = 0.5 design target, and its `Q_array` = 0.0002245 is 26x short of the `M*Q_H0` = 0.005946 the sizing law asks for. Reaching `H0` at all needs `N` = 36; reaching it with DR-0007's declared `M` = 1.5 margin needs `N` = 53. What `N = 2` is *not* is off by orders of magnitude in entropy -- the bound's saturation means the entropy shortfall is 0.080 bit, not 0.5 bit. Both statements are true and the second is the one that makes this a design trade rather than a failure.
